# Entry Protocols Across Human Institutions
## Mapping Institutional Arrival Patterns to Agent Onboarding

*Assessment date: 2026-02-23*
*Project: Cellar Door — Agent Lifecycle Protocols*

---

## 1. Immigration

### Documentation that travels
- Passport (identity + nationality attestation by home state)
- Visa (pre-authorization from destination state)
- Health records, vaccination certificates
- Criminal background checks (some jurisdictions)
- Proof of funds, employment letters, sponsorship documents
- Refugee: UNHCR registration, persecution evidence

### Who decides / criteria
- **Consular officers** (visa issuance) — sovereign discretion, nearly unreviewable
- **Border agents** (entry) — can deny even valid visa holders
- Criteria: security risk, health risk, economic burden, criminal history, ties to home country (intent to return), quota/category limits

### Verification
- Biometrics (fingerprints, facial recognition) checked against watchlists
- Document authentication (holograms, chips, database lookups)
- Interview at consulate and/or border
- Advanced Passenger Information shared before arrival
- Five Eyes / Interpol data sharing

### Conditions on entry
- Duration limits, geographic restrictions
- Employment restrictions (tourist vs work visa)
- Reporting requirements (check-in with authorities)
- Quarantine (health emergencies)
- Bond/financial guarantees
- Conditional residence leading to permanent status

### Rights of arriving entity vs receiving institution
- Asylum seekers: non-refoulement (cannot be returned to persecution)
- Due process rights vary enormously by jurisdiction
- Visa holders: generally can be expelled for violation of conditions
- Citizens: near-absolute right of entry to own country
- Receiving state: sovereign right to exclude non-citizens

### Fraud prevention
- Machine-readable travel documents, biometric passports
- Centralized databases (ESTA, ETIAS, SIS II)
- Carrier sanctions (airlines check documents pre-boarding)
- Document forensics at border

### Appeal process
- Visa denial: limited or no appeal in most jurisdictions (reapply)
- Entry denial at border: varies; often minimal due process for non-citizens
- Asylum denial: typically some administrative/judicial review
- Deportation: hearing rights in many jurisdictions

### Temporal properties
- Visa validity windows, entry/exit dates
- 90/180-day rules (Schengen)
- Cooling-off periods between visa types
- Path to permanent residency → citizenship (years-long progression)
- Conditional permanent residence (2-year marriage green card)
- Probationary naturalization periods

## 2. Corporate Onboarding

### Documentation that travels
- Resume/CV (self-attested history)
- References from prior employers
- Educational credentials
- Professional certifications
- Background check consent + results
- Employment eligibility documents (I-9, right-to-work)
- Non-compete / IP assignment history
- Portfolio of work (for some roles)

### Who decides / criteria
- Hiring manager + HR + sometimes committee
- Criteria: skills match, cultural fit, background check pass, reference validation, compensation alignment
- Executive hires: board approval

### Verification
- Reference checks (calling prior employers)
- Background checks (criminal, credit, education verification)
- Drug screening
- Skills assessments / technical interviews
- Credential verification services

### Conditions on entry
- Probationary period (30-90 days typical, sometimes 6 months)
- Restricted system access initially
- Training completion requirements
- Confidentiality agreements, IP assignment
- Non-compete (jurisdiction-dependent enforceability)
- Security clearance pending (for defense sector)

### Rights of arriving entity vs receiving institution
- At-will employment (US): either party can terminate
- Probationary employees: reduced protections in many jurisdictions
- Anti-discrimination protections apply from day 1
- Company: can define role, access, expectations
- Employee: can leave, owns own skills/reputation

### Fraud prevention
- Background checks, reference verification
- Credential verification services
- Probationary periods as real-world testing
- E-Verify (employment eligibility)

### Appeal process
- Failed background check: dispute process (FCRA rights)
- Rescinded offer: limited recourse unless discriminatory
- Probation termination: typically minimal appeal rights
- Union environments: grievance procedures

### Temporal properties
- Probation (30-180 days)
- Vesting schedules (equity, retirement)
- Benefits eligibility waiting periods
- Progressive access expansion over time
- Annual reviews as continued credentialing

## 3. Academic Admissions

### Documentation that travels
- Transcripts (official, sealed)
- Standardized test scores
- Letters of recommendation
- Personal statements / essays
- Transfer credit evaluations
- International: credential evaluation (WES, ECE)
- Portfolio (arts programs)
- Language proficiency scores (TOEFL, IELTS)

### Who decides / criteria
- Admissions committee (faculty + professional admissions staff)
- Criteria: academic record, test scores, fit, diversity goals, capacity
- Transfer: course equivalency determined by registrar/department
- Graduate: faculty advisor willingness

### Verification
- Official transcripts sent institution-to-institution
- Test scores sent directly from testing agencies
- Credential evaluation services for international documents
- National Student Clearinghouse (US enrollment verification)
- Plagiarism detection on application materials

### Conditions on entry
- Conditional admission (pending final grades, language proficiency)
- Prerequisite courses required
- Academic probation from start (low GPA admits)
- Enrollment deposit / commitment deadline
- Orientation completion requirements
- Transfer credit limits (max transferable hours)

### Rights of arriving entity vs receiving institution
- Student: FERPA privacy rights, due process for disciplinary actions
- Institution: sets curriculum, standards, codes of conduct
- Accreditation bodies: constrain what institutions can do
- Financial aid creates additional obligations on both sides

### Fraud prevention
- Official transcript protocols (sealed, direct transmission)
- Credential evaluation by recognized agencies
- Plagiarism detection
- Interview (selective programs)
- Application verification services

### Appeal process
- Admission denial: typically no formal appeal (reapply)
- Transfer credit disputes: petition process through registrar
- Some institutions allow appeal with new information
- Grade appeals have separate processes

### Temporal properties
- Application deadlines, decision timelines
- Enrollment deposits with deadlines
- Conditional admission expiration
- Credit transfer age limits (courses older than X years may not transfer)
- Degree completion time limits
- Academic probation windows

## 4. Medical Credentialing

### Documentation that travels
- Medical license(s) — state-specific
- Board certifications
- DEA registration
- Medical school diploma, residency completion
- Malpractice history (NPDB — National Practitioner Data Bank)
- CME (Continuing Medical Education) records
- Peer references
- Procedure/case logs
- Malpractice insurance certificates

### Who decides / criteria
- Hospital credentialing committee (physicians)
- Medical staff office (administrative processing)
- State medical board (licensure)
- Insurance panels (separate credentialing)
- Criteria: training adequacy, competence, malpractice history, peer assessment, need for specialty

### Verification
- Primary source verification (contact each issuing institution directly)
- NPDB queries (mandatory)
- State license board checks (every state of prior practice)
- AMA Physician Masterfile
- Federation of State Medical Boards (FSMB) profile
- OIG exclusion list check
- CAQH ProView (standardized credentialing application)

### Conditions on entry
- Provisional/temporary privileges (limited duration)
- Proctoring requirements (observed cases)
- Case volume minimums
- Specific procedure privileges (not blanket)
- Focused Professional Practice Evaluation (FPPE) — mandatory for all new privileges
- Supervision requirements for new physicians

### Rights of arriving entity vs receiving institution
- Physician: due process rights for privilege denial/revocation (Health Care Quality Improvement Act)
- Hospital: can set standards above minimum licensure
- Fair hearing rights if adverse action taken
- Reporting obligations to NPDB, state boards
- Anti-competitive concerns (sham peer review)

### Fraud prevention
- Primary source verification (gold standard — go to original source)
- Multiple overlapping databases
- Criminal background checks
- Drug testing
- ECFMG verification for international graduates
- Continuous monitoring (ongoing OIG, sanctions checks)

### Appeal process
- Denial of privileges: fair hearing before peer committee
- State license denial: administrative hearing
- NPDB reports: dispute process
- Detailed appellate rights mandated by federal law

### Temporal properties
- Credentialing cycle: typically 2-year re-credentialing
- Provisional privileges: 90-120 days typical
- FPPE period: first cases at new institution
- Ongoing Professional Practice Evaluation (OPPE) — continuous
- License renewal cycles
- Board recertification (every 10 years, shifting to continuous)

## 5. Prison Transfers

### Documentation that travels
- Pre-sentence investigation report
- Sentence computation records
- Medical/mental health records
- Disciplinary history
- Classification assessments (risk scores)
- Detainers and warrants
- Program completion records
- Gang intelligence files
- Protective custody status
- Victim notification obligations

### Who decides / criteria
- Interstate Compact for Adult Offender Supervision (ICAOS) — for community supervision transfers
- Bureau of Prisons designation staff — for federal transfers
- State DOC classification units
- Criteria: security level, bed space, programming needs, safety concerns, proximity to family, separatees (co-defendants who must be kept apart)

### Verification
- Direct institution-to-institution records transfer
- NCIC checks, warrant checks
- Fingerprint verification on arrival
- Medical screening on arrival (TB, communicable disease)
- Mental health assessment

### Conditions on entry
- Reclassification upon arrival (receiving institution may change security level)
- Orientation period (institutional quarantine)
- Program waitlists
- Cell/housing assignment based on risk
- Restricted movement initially
- Medical clearance required

### Rights of arriving entity vs receiving institution
- Prisoner: constitutional minimum conditions (8th Amendment)
- Due process for classification decisions (limited)
- Right to medical care continues
- Receiving institution: broad discretion on housing, programming
- Cannot worsen conditions arbitrarily as punishment for transfer

### Fraud prevention
- Photo ID, fingerprinting, biometrics
- Escort during transfer
- Direct institution-to-institution communication
- U.S. Marshals Service for federal movement
- Chain of custody documentation

### Appeal process
- Classification decisions: administrative grievance process
- Transfer decisions: very limited review (generally administrative discretion)
- Conditions: grievance → administrative exhaustion → § 1983 litigation

### Temporal properties
- Receiving/orientation period (14-30 days)
- Reclassification timeline
- Good time recalculation
- Program enrollment waiting periods
- Minimum time at security level before reclassification

## 6. Military Transfers (PCS)

### Documentation that travels
- PCS orders (Permanent Change of Station)
- Service record / personnel file (Official Military Personnel File)
- Medical records (now largely electronic — AHLTA/MHS Genesis)
- Security clearance (JPAS/DISS)
- Training records, qualifications, certifications
- Fitness reports / evaluations (OERs, NCOERs, FITREPs)
- Awards and decorations
- Financial records (pay, allowances)
- Family member documentation (DEERS enrollment)
- Unit clearance checklist (signed out of losing unit)

### Who decides / criteria
- Human Resources Command / Personnel Management (service-specific)
- Needs of the service (primary criterion)
- Career progression requirements
- Qualifications match
- Security clearance compatibility
- Humanitarian considerations (secondary)

### Verification
- CAC (Common Access Card) — smart card with PKI certificates
- DEERS database verification
- Security clearance reciprocity (supposed to be automatic across DoD)
- Medical readiness verification (Individual Medical Readiness)
- Training record verification through service databases

### Conditions on entry
- In-processing checklist (standardized per installation)
- Gaining unit sponsorship program
- Area orientation, safety briefings
- Report-no-later-than date
- Assumption of duties timeline
- Required training completion (local threats, regulations)

### Rights of arriving entity vs receiving institution
- Service member: assignment according to orders; limited choice
- Gaining unit: can assign duties within specialty; cannot reject valid transfer
- UCMJ applies throughout; chain of command transfers
- Cannot be denied entry based on gaining unit preference (orders are orders)

### Fraud prevention
- CAC/PKI infrastructure (cryptographic identity)
- Centralized personnel databases
- Orders verification through official channels
- Security clearance verified in DISS
- Chain of command authentication

### Appeal process
- Assignment changes: request through chain of command
- Inspector General complaints
- Congressional inquiry
- Board for Correction of Military Records
- Article 138 complaints (UCMJ)

### Temporal properties
- Report-no-later-than date
- Permissive TDY for house hunting
- Transition leave
- Gaining unit sponsorship begins before arrival
- 90-day "honeymoon" period (informal)
- First evaluation timeline

## 7. Banking / KYC

### Documentation that travels
- Government-issued ID
- Proof of address
- Tax identification number (SSN, TIN, equivalent)
- Source of funds documentation
- Business registration documents (entities)
- Beneficial ownership information
- Correspondent banking: SWIFT/BIC codes, nostro/vostro account relationships, KYC packages between banks

### Who decides / criteria
- Compliance department (BSA/AML officer)
- Automated risk scoring systems
- Criteria: identity verification, risk level (PEP status, country risk, industry risk), regulatory requirements (FATF, FinCEN), profitability
- Correspondent banking: FATF mutual evaluation, country risk, relationship profitability

### Verification
- Document verification (manual + automated)
- Database checks (OFAC/SDN list, PEP databases, adverse media)
- Credit bureau checks
- Biometric verification (increasingly)
- Enhanced Due Diligence for high-risk
- Correspondent banking: Wolfsberg questionnaire, on-site visits, SWIFT KYC Registry

### Conditions on entry
- Transaction limits initially
- Enhanced monitoring period
- Restricted product access
- Hold periods on deposits
- Periodic re-verification (ongoing KYC)
- Correspondent banking: transaction corridors, nested account prohibitions

### Rights of arriving entity vs receiving institution
- Customer: right to non-discriminatory access (CRA), right to know why denied
- Privacy rights (GLBA)
- Bank: right to refuse service (BSA/AML obligations can mandate refusal)
- De-risking tensions (entire countries/industries cut off)
- No general "right to a bank account" in US (some EU protections exist)

### Fraud prevention
- Multi-factor identity verification
- Ongoing transaction monitoring
- Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs)
- Information sharing (314(b) program)
- Beneficial ownership requirements (CTA)
- Biometrics, liveness detection

### Appeal process
- Account denial: limited formal recourse
- Can request reason for denial
- Regulatory complaints (CFPB, OCC)
- ChexSystems disputes
- No general right to appeal

### Temporal properties
- Account opening: immediate to days (depending on verification)
- Hold periods on initial deposits (Regulation CC)
- 90-day enhanced monitoring (typical for new accounts)
- Periodic KYC refresh (1-3 year cycle based on risk)
- Correspondent banking: annual review cycle

## 8. Domain Name Transfers

### Documentation that travels
- Auth code (EPP authorization code / transfer key)
- WHOIS data (registrant identity)
- Domain registration records
- DNS configuration
- Transfer lock status

### Who decides / criteria
- Gaining registrar processes the request
- Losing registrar must release (cannot unreasonably block)
- ICANN transfer policy governs
- Registrant authorization required
- Criteria: valid auth code, domain unlocked, not within 60-day lock period, not in dispute

### Verification
- Auth code validation (shared secret between registrant and registry)
- Registrant email confirmation (FOA — Form of Authorization)
- WHOIS data match
- Gaining registrar identity verification of requesting party

### Conditions on entry
- 60-day post-registration/transfer lock (cannot transfer again)
- 1-year registration extension upon transfer
- DNS propagation period
- Gaining registrar's terms of service apply

### Rights of arriving entity vs receiving institution
- Registrant: owns the domain; registrar is custodian
- ICANN policies protect registrant rights
- Gaining registrar: must comply with ICANN transfer policies
- Cannot refuse valid transfer request
- Registrant can switch registrars freely (after lock periods)

### Fraud prevention
- Auth code (possession-based authentication)
- Email verification to registrant of record
- 60-day lock after WHOIS changes (prevent hijack-then-transfer)
- Registrar lock (clientTransferProhibited)
- Registry lock (for high-value domains)

### Appeal process
- ICANN Transfer Dispute Resolution Policy
- UDRP (Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy) for ownership disputes
- Losing registrar can deny transfer only for specific ICANN-approved reasons
- Gaining registrar must comply with ICANN timelines

### Temporal properties
- 60-day post-registration lock
- 60-day post-WHOIS-change lock
- 5-day transfer processing window
- Auto-approval if losing registrar doesn't respond
- 1-year extension on successful transfer
- 30-day grace period after expiration

## 9. Professional Licensing

### Documentation that travels
- License from originating jurisdiction
- Exam scores (bar exam, PE exam, NCLEX)
- Education transcripts
- Continuing education records
- Disciplinary history
- Character and fitness evaluation results
- Supervised practice records
- Specialty certifications

### Who decides / criteria
- Receiving jurisdiction's licensing board
- Criteria varies enormously:
  - **Bar admission**: character & fitness, reciprocity/comity rules, UBE score portability, motion admission (years of practice)
  - **Engineering PE**: NCEES record, comity application, state-specific requirements
  - **Nursing**: Nurse Licensure Compact (NLC) — multi-state license, 40 states
  - **Medicine**: IMLC (Interstate Medical Licensure Compact) — expedited, not automatic

### Verification
- Originating board verification (direct contact)
- NCEES records (engineering)
- NURSYS database (nursing)
- National Conference of Bar Examiners
- Disciplinary database checks
- Criminal background checks

### Conditions on entry
- Supervised practice period (some jurisdictions)
- Additional exams (state-specific portions)
- Jurisprudence exam (state law knowledge)
- Continuing education specific to new jurisdiction
- Practice area restrictions (conditional license)
- Temporary/provisional license while processing

### Rights of arriving entity vs receiving institution
- License holder: constitutional right to practice profession (substantive due process)
- Receiving board: police power to regulate professions
- Compact members: agreed-upon reciprocity terms
- Cannot impose unreasonable barriers (antitrust / dormant commerce clause)

### Fraud prevention
- Board-to-board verification
- Centralized databases
- Criminal background checks
- Character and fitness investigations
- Exam score verification from testing agencies

### Appeal process
- License denial: administrative hearing
- Judicial review of board decisions
- Due process protections (14th Amendment — property interest in license)
- Compact-specific dispute resolution

### Temporal properties
- Application processing (weeks to months)
- Temporary practice permits while processing
- Continuing education cycles
- Renewal periods (annual, biennial)
- Supervision periods for conditional admission
- Reciprocity eligibility requirements (years of practice in originating jurisdiction)

## 10. Digital Identity

### Documentation that travels
- **SAML**: Assertion (XML signed document) — identity + attributes + authentication statement
- **OIDC**: ID Token (JWT) — claims about identity, signed by IdP
- **Verifiable Credentials**: W3C VC (JSON-LD or JWT) — claims by issuer about subject, holder-controlled
- **X.509 certificates**: PKI chain of trust
- **OAuth tokens**: access/refresh tokens (authorization, not identity per se)

### Who decides / criteria
- **Relying Party (RP)** / Service Provider (SP) decides whether to accept
- Criteria: trust in the Identity Provider, attribute sufficiency, authentication level (LoA/AAL), freshness of assertion
- Federation policies (InCommon, eduGAIN, STORK) set minimum standards
- Trust frameworks (NIST 800-63, eIDAS) define assurance levels

### Verification
- Cryptographic signature verification (IdP signed the assertion)
- Certificate chain validation
- Token introspection endpoints
- Revocation checking (CRL, OCSP, status lists for VCs)
- Audience/recipient validation
- Replay protection (nonce, timestamp)

### Conditions on entry
- Scoped attributes (only what's needed — minimal disclosure)
- Session duration limits
- Step-up authentication for sensitive operations
- Attribute release policies (IdP controls what's shared)
- Consent requirements (GDPR, privacy regulations)
- Terms of service acceptance

### Rights of arriving entity vs receiving institution
- User: data minimization rights, consent withdrawal, right to be forgotten (GDPR)
- User controls credential presentation (especially VCs — holder-centric)
- RP: can set authentication requirements, can refuse
- IdP: mediates trust, can revoke
- Federation: governance framework binds all parties

### Fraud prevention
- Cryptographic binding (assertions can't be forged without IdP's key)
- Channel binding (proof of possession)
- Replay protection (nonce, short-lived tokens)
- Multi-factor authentication requirements
- Continuous authentication signals
- Holder binding (biometric or device binding for VCs)
- Revocation mechanisms

### Appeal process
- Account recovery processes at IdP
- Federation arbitration for disputes between IdP and RP
- No universal appeal — depends on federation governance
- GDPR data access/rectification requests

### Temporal properties
- Token/assertion lifetime (minutes to hours — short-lived by design)
- Refresh token rotation
- Session timeout and re-authentication
- Credential expiration
- Revocation latency (how fast can a revoked credential be detected?)
- Federation metadata refresh intervals
- Trust framework certification cycles

## Synthesis: Cross-Institutional Patterns

### Universal Properties of Entry Protocols

| Property | Description | Prevalence |
|----------|-------------|------------|
| **Identity attestation** | Some trusted party vouches for who you are | Universal |
| **Documentation bundle** | A set of records travels with the entity | Universal |
| **Gatekeeping authority** | Someone has power to admit or deny | Universal |
| **Verification against source** | Claims are checked, ideally at primary source | Universal |
| **Conditional entry** | Provisional status with restrictions | 9/10 |
| **Probationary period** | Time-limited reduced trust | 9/10 |
| **Fraud detection** | Active measures against impersonation | Universal |
| **Progressive trust** | Rights/access expand over time | 8/10 |
| **Appeal/dispute** | Some mechanism to contest denial | 8/10 (varies in strength) |
| **Temporal gates** | Cooling-off periods, lock periods, wait times | Universal |
| **Bilateral obligations** | Both parties take on responsibilities | Universal |
| **Continuous monitoring** | Ongoing evaluation after admission | 7/10 |

### Key Structural Observations

1. **Primary source verification is the gold standard.** Medical credentialing does this most rigorously. Immigration and digital identity use cryptographic equivalents. Corporate onboarding is surprisingly weak.

2. **Probation is universal but implementation varies wildly.** From 90-day employment probation (easy termination) to years-long conditional residence (deportation risk). The question isn't whether to have probation but what constraints apply during it.

3. **The losing institution's cooperation matters enormously.** Domain transfers require the losing registrar. Medical credentialing requires every prior hospital. Military transfers require unit clearance. Entry protocols that don't account for the sending party's behavior are incomplete.

4. **Documentation bundles are institution-specific and non-portable.** There's no universal "transfer packet." Every receiving institution wants different things in different formats. This is a major friction point.

5. **Trust is not transitive.** Hospital A trusting Dr. Smith does not mean Hospital B will. Bar admission in New York doesn't mean you can practice in California. Trust must be re-established at each entry point, though compacts and federations reduce this friction.

6. **Fraud prevention correlates with stakes, not sophistication.** Domain transfers (auth codes + email) are simpler than medical credentialing (primary source verification of every credential). The rigor scales with consequences of failure.

7. **The appeal asymmetry is stark.** Citizens have strong entry rights; non-citizens have weak ones. Licensed professionals have due process; job applicants don't. The more an entity has already invested, the stronger the appeal rights.

8. **Continuous monitoring is the trend.** KYC refresh cycles, OPPE in medicine, ongoing security clearance review. One-time entry verification is being replaced by continuous evaluation everywhere.

## Agent ENTRY Protocol Properties

Drawing from the institutional analysis, an agent ENTRY protocol should consider:

### Tier 1 — Do Now (Technical Foundation)

1. **Identity attestation**
   - Cryptographic identity (key pair, signed credentials)
   - Provenance chain: who created this agent, what model, what version
   - Analogous to: passport + visa, CAC, X.509 certificate

2. **Capability declaration**
   - What can this agent do? What tools does it have access to?
   - What permissions does it request vs. what is granted?
   - Analogous to: visa category (tourist vs. work), medical privilege delineation

3. **Scoped authorization**
   - Least-privilege by default
   - Explicit capability grants, not ambient authority
   - Analogous to: conditional admission, restricted license, probationary access

4. **Probationary period**
   - Reduced trust initially, expanded based on behavior
   - Enhanced monitoring during probation
   - Analogous to: employment probation, FPPE, conditional residence

5. **Documentation bundle**
   - Standardized "transfer packet" for agent migration
   - State, memory, configuration, credential chain
   - Analogous to: PCS orders + personnel file, medical credentialing packet

6. **Revocation / kill switch**
   - Receiving system can terminate entry at any time during probation
   - Clean shutdown protocol
   - Analogous to: visa revocation, privilege suspension, domain transfer cancellation

### Tier 2 — Do When Legal Framework Exists

7. **Bilateral obligations contract**
   - What does the receiving system owe the agent? (resource commitments, data handling)
   - What does the agent owe the receiving system? (behavioral constraints, reporting)
   - Analogous to: employment contract, terms of service, federation agreement

8. **Appeal / dispute resolution**
   - Process for contesting entry denial or mid-entry termination
   - Escalation path (automated → human review)
   - Analogous to: asylum hearing, fair hearing rights, ICANN TDRP

9. **Continuous monitoring framework**
   - Ongoing behavioral evaluation, not just point-in-time entry check
   - Re-credentialing cycles
   - Analogous to: OPPE, KYC refresh, security clearance periodic reinvestigation

10. **Cross-system credential portability**
    - Agent credentials recognized across platforms/systems
    - Compact/federation model for participating systems
    - Analogous to: nursing compact, SAML federation, ICAO passport standards

11. **Sending system obligations**
    - Outgoing system must provide clean handoff
    - Cannot sabotage transfer
    - Analogous to: losing registrar obligations, unit clearance checklist

### Tier 3 — Do When Resources Allow

12. **Reputation portability**
    - Behavioral history travels with agent (privacy-preserving)
    - Analogous to: NPDB, credit scores, professional references

13. **Graduated trust frameworks**
    - Multi-level assurance (like NIST AAL levels)
    - Different entry requirements for different trust tiers
    - Analogous to: eIDAS assurance levels, security clearance tiers

14. **Inter-agent entry negotiation**
    - Agents negotiating entry terms with other agents (no human in loop)
    - Automated credentialing
    - Analogous to: correspondent banking, automated certificate validation

15. **Ecosystem-level governance**
    - Standards body for entry protocols
    - Accreditation of receiving systems
    - Analogous to: ICANN, FATF, medical accreditation bodies (Joint Commission)

## Novel Properties for AI Agents (No Human Analog)

1. **Clonability / instance ambiguity**
   - Humans can't be in two places at once. Agents can. Entry protocol must handle: Is this the same agent or a copy? Do copies inherit credentials? What happens to entry status when an agent forks?
   - No institutional analog exists. Closest: franchise licensing, but that's a stretch.

2. **State mutability**
   - An agent's "character" can change between entry points (fine-tuning, prompt modification, tool changes). A doctor is still a doctor between hospitals; an agent might be fundamentally different.
   - Entry protocol needs: state hash verification, behavioral fingerprinting, drift detection.

3. **Non-continuous identity**
   - Agents may be instantiated, destroyed, re-instantiated. There's no persistent biological continuity.
   - Entry protocol needs: identity anchored to something other than continuity of consciousness.

4. **Capability explosion**
   - A human licensed as a doctor can't suddenly become a lawyer mid-session. An agent given a new tool can suddenly do things it couldn't before.
   - Entry protocol needs: runtime capability monitoring, not just point-in-time credentialing.

5. **Transparent internals (potentially)**
   - You can inspect an agent's weights, prompts, tool access in ways you can never inspect a human's thoughts.
   - Entry protocol could leverage: model card verification, system prompt attestation, tool inventory signing.

6. **Speed of action**
   - An agent can cause damage in milliseconds. Human entry protocols assume human-speed action.
   - Entry protocol needs: real-time behavioral monitoring, circuit breakers, rate limiting as a first-class entry condition.

7. **No inherent rights**
   - Humans have natural/constitutional rights that constrain entry protocols. Agents (currently) have none.
   - This is both a simplification (fewer constraints on protocol design) and a risk (no floor on how badly agents can be treated, creating race-to-the-bottom dynamics).

8. **Provenance chain matters more than reputation**
   - For humans, lived reputation matters. For agents, who-made-this and what-it's-made-of matters more than behavioral history, because behavior can be reprogrammed.
   - Entry protocol needs: signed model provenance, training data attestation, system prompt integrity verification.

## ENTRY Risks That EXIT Doesn't Have

| Risk | Why ENTRY has it | Why EXIT doesn't |
|------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Trojan horse** | Malicious agent entering disguised as benign | Exiting agent already inside; damage already possible |
| **Credential forgery** | Fake credentials to gain unauthorized entry | Exit doesn't require credentials |
| **Capability smuggling** | Hidden tools/capabilities not declared at entry | Exit doesn't grant new capabilities |
| **State injection** | Corrupted/poisoned state introduced to new environment | Exit removes state from environment |
| **Trust bootstrap problem** | No prior relationship to base trust on | Exit has accumulated trust history |
| **Resource exhaustion on arrival** | Newcomer consumes resources before proving value | Exiting entity frees resources |
| **Social engineering of entry** | Manipulating gatekeepers to bypass entry controls | Exit gatekeepers have less to protect against |
| **Incompatible assumptions** | Agent's training/values misaligned with new environment | Known quantity by exit time |
| **Cascading trust** | Entry to system A used to claim entry to system B | Exit from A doesn't grant entry to B |
| **Quarantine evasion** | Bypassing probationary restrictions | No quarantine on exit |

### The Fundamental Asymmetry

Entry is about **accepting unknown risk**. Exit is about **managing known loss**. This makes entry protocols inherently harder to design correctly because:

1. You're making decisions under uncertainty (how will this agent behave?)
2. The cost of a wrong entry decision is potentially catastrophic (malicious agent inside your system)
3. The cost of a wrong exit decision is bounded (agent is leaving; damage is limited to the transition)
4. Entry fraud is high-value (gaining unauthorized access); exit fraud is lower-value (what do you gain by faking an exit?)

The one area where exit is harder: **preventing data exfiltration during exit**. An agent leaving with your data is an entry-like problem in reverse. But the agent was already trusted enough to have access — the failure was in ongoing monitoring, not in exit protocol design.

---

## Recommendations for Cellar Door

1. **Start with the medical credentialing model** as the primary reference architecture. It has the most rigorous verification (primary source), mandatory probation (FPPE), ongoing monitoring (OPPE), and strong due process. It's the closest analog to what agent entry should look like.

2. **Borrow the digital identity stack** for implementation. OIDC/VC patterns for credential exchange, cryptographic verification, scoped attributes, short-lived tokens. Don't reinvent what already works.

3. **Add the novel AI properties** as a layer on top. Clonability controls, capability monitoring, state verification — these have no analog and need first-principles design.

4. **Design for the asymmetry.** Entry is harder than exit. Invest disproportionately in entry protocol rigor. The cost of over-trusting at entry is much higher than the cost of over-restricting at exit.

5. **Build compacts, not universal standards.** The nursing compact and SAML federation models show that bilateral/multilateral agreements between willing parties work better than top-down universal standards. Start with a small federation of cooperating systems.
