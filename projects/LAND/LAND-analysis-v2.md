### Doc 5: REPORT_north_american_mineral_rights.md (~33K)
A legal primer that became a strategic filter. Key revelation: **unpatented US mining claims are a trap** — they grant extraction rights only, no land ownership, no non-mining commercial use. The "dungeon core" vision is legally impossible on unpatented claims.

**The taxonomy that matters:**
- US Unpatented Claims: right to mine on federal land only. NO storage, NO tourism, NO agriculture. Violation = forfeiture.
- US Patented Claims: full private property (surface + subsurface). No new patents since 1994. Rare legacy assets.
- US Fee-Simple with intact minerals: the gold standard. Complete private ownership.
- Canadian Freehold Subsurface: perpetual, privately-owned void space. The Pitt Meadows asset.
- Canadian Crown Leases: temporary, improvements revert at lease end. Unsuitable for long-term investment.

**Case studies analyzed:** Vernon & Snowstorm (NV, $3,997 — unpatented, PASS), Vanderbilt Mine (AZ, $53K — unpatented, PASS), Bella Vista (CA, $139K — split estate, minerals reserved by government, PASS), Hiawatha Farm (KS, $1.25M — fee-simple with minerals, EXCELLENT), Hatfield Creek (TN, $72K — fee-simple with minerals, EXCELLENT).

**Go-forward filter:** Only target fee-simple with intact minerals, patented claims, or Canadian freehold subsurface. Everything else is eliminated.

## 9. Legal/Regulatory — BC-Specific

### Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR)
- Covers most of the Lower Mainland including Pitt Meadows surface land
- Non-farm use requires ALC application — approval is discretionary, not guaranteed
- "Sweetener" strategy: propose net-positive agricultural outcomes (underground mushroom farming, improved drainage)
- ALC panels are political bodies; social license matters as much as legal arguments

### Mining Claims & Mineral Tenure Act (MTA)
- Freehold mineral rights are rare legacy assets from pre-reservation Crown Grants
- "Mineral" definition excludes sand, gravel, peat, soil, petroleum, natural gas
- Even hand-tool exploration requires a Notice of Work
- New claims since March 2025 require First Nations consultation before grant
- Mine Manager designation required for any operation (can be the owner)

### Floodplain Regulation
- Pitt Meadows: Flood Construction Level ~5.75m GSC
- All structures must have floors above FCL
- Critical equipment must be above FCL
- Shaft collar must be designed as watertight, flood-proof structure

### First Nations Consultation
- Constitutional duty to consult exists for any Crown decision (permits, approvals)
- BC's new MCCF framework adds time and complexity to mineral claim applications
- Not just procedural — meaningful accommodation may be required
- Failure to consult properly can invalidate permits

### Zoning
- M3 Heavy Industrial (Quesnel): broad permissions, permits virtually any industrial use
- ALR-overlay properties: non-farm use application required even for permitted activities
- Residential use: accessory only to industrial (Quesnel); not permitted on mining claims
- Underground: largely unzoned but subject to Mines Act and Building Code for human-rated spaces
