## Do I Understand What This Is?

Yes. EXIT.protocol is a proposed open standard for **portable departure records for AI agents** — cryptographically signed receipts that let an AI agent prove where it's been, why it left, and what its track record looks like, without calling home to the old platform. Think "Carfax report for AI agents." The vendor behind it is called **Cellar Door**.

The site itself is a five-mode slider that presents the same concept through five lenses: Poet, Idealist, Pragmatist, Bureaucrat, and Agent. That's the gimmick — same protocol, different audiences.

## Is There a Story?

**Yes, but it's not the protocol.** The protocol is early (v0.1, SDK alpha, selective disclosure "planned"). There's no shipping product to review. The story angles:

1. **The "agent rights" framing is the real lede.** Letters of manumission, Nansen passports, the Underground Railroad — they're explicitly framing AI agent mobility as a *civil rights* issue. That's a bold, potentially inflammatory claim that would generate clicks and debate. Last time I said they bury this. They still do — it's in the Idealist tab, not front-and-center.

2. **The five-voice site is a neat UX trick** — but it's a design story, not a tech story. Writing the same spec as poetry, policy brief, and terminal output is clever. Worth a screenshot in a roundup.

3. **The actual technical question — should AI agents have portable reputation? — is genuinely interesting** and timely, given the EU AI Act references and the explosion of autonomous agent frameworks. But Cellar Door doesn't appear to have traction, users, or even a shipped SDK yet.

## What Headline Would I Write?

> **"This startup thinks AI agents deserve passports — and it's not entirely wrong"**

Alt:
> **"EXIT.protocol wants to give AI agents the right to leave (and take their reputation with them)"**

## Verdict: **Skip (for now) — Revisit when SDK ships**

**Reasoning:** The concept is genuinely novel and the framing is provocative enough for a feature — but there's nothing to *test*. No shipped SDK, no real-world deployments, no agent actually using this. The five-voice site is slick but it's still a pitch deck dressed as a product page. When they have a working demo with real agents creating real markers, that's a story. Right now it's a spec and a vibe.

**Changed from "Cover in a roundup" to "Skip" because:** A roundup implies there are comparable things to round up with it. There aren't — this is too sui generis for a listicle. It's either a standalone feature or nothing. And without a shipped product, it's nothing yet.

**Flag for:** Q2 2026 revisit. If they ship the SDK + get one platform to integrate, instant feature.
