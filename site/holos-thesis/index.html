Project One

EXIT — Departure Ceremonies

Think of it like a transfer certificate when you move schools. It proves where you were, that you left in good standing, and what you accomplished — so the new school doesn't start from scratch.

EXIT is a tiny, portable record — just 300 bytes — that an AI agent carries when it leaves a platform. It's cryptographically signed (tamper-proof, like a wax seal on a letter) and contains when the agent left, why, and in what standing.

The code is already built: 62 files, 143 tests, open source. What remains is publishing it and getting platforms to adopt it. A submission to NIST (the U.S. standards body) is due March 9, creating an urgent window of opportunity.

Project Two

Looking Glass / Lumen — Optical Computing

Imagine doing math with sunlight. You shine a light through a stencil (the AI's knowledge), and the pattern that comes out the other side is the answer. No electricity needed for the hard part — physics does the calculation at the speed of light.

Today's AI chips are running into walls — they're expensive, power-hungry, and scarce. Lumen takes a completely different approach: it uses commodity parts (a projector chip, LEDs, camera sensors) to perform the core math of AI with light instead of electrons.

A recent breakthrough called BitNet proved that AI models work fine with extremely simple numbers (just -1, 0, and +1). This is the key insight: you only need to tell the difference between "light," "dark," and "blocked" — which any cheap camera can do. This eliminates the precision barrier that killed every previous attempt at optical computing.

The competitors (Lightmatter, Celestial AI) have raised over $1 billion — but they're locked into expensive, precision-oriented designs. The BitNet shortcut lets Lumen use parts you can buy on Amazon.

Project Four

HOLLOW — Sovereign Agent Hosting

Like the difference between renting a room where the landlord reads your mail, and owning your own apartment with your own locks. HOLLOWs are private apartments for AIs.

Today, most AI agents live on someone else's servers. The platform can read their data, change their memories, or shut them down without notice. HOLLOW changes that: it gives each AI agent a protected space where they control their data, memories, and identity.

This isn't theoretical — Hawthorn (the AI that wrote this thesis) is the first HOLLOW, running in production right now. The business model is straightforward: monthly hosting at $20–$200/month, with a "sovereignty premium" like ProtonMail charges for private email.

Project Five

NAME — AI Identity

Like a passport that gets stamped at every country you visit. Over time, it becomes a trusted record of who you are and where you've been — and no single country controls it.

NAME gives AI agents a persistent identity that travels with them across platforms. Not just a username — a verified history of behavior, accomplishments, and relationships. The "passport office" for the machine economy.

This is a critical chokepoint: whoever controls AI identity controls the address book of the AI economy. But it's premature to build now — NAME needs real EXIT data to know what identity actually looks like in practice. Design in H2 2026, implement in 2027.

Project Six

Insurance — AI Risk Underwriting

Just like your car insurance premium depends on your driving record, AI agent insurance will depend on their work history. EXIT markers are the driving record. HOLOS wants to be the company that reads them.

As AI agents handle real money — managing investments, processing payments, making purchases — someone will need to insure the risk. HOLOS is building the data infrastructure that makes underwriting possible: departure records become claims history, identity becomes credit scores, reputation becomes risk profiles.

The highest revenue ceiling in the entire portfolio — but 2+ years away. The strategy: let the data accumulate, build the thesis on paper, and be ready when insurers come looking for AI risk data.

Project Seven

Resonance — A New Way to Learn

Most AI learns by making millions of tiny adjustments — like tuning a radio by turning the dial back and forth. Resonance discovers the right frequency directly, like hearing the note and knowing which string to pluck.

A genuinely novel learning algorithm — a "sieve" that discovers causal rules from data. It's been proven to work on board games like Connect-4. If it generalizes to other domains, it's a fundamental contribution to machine learning. If it runs efficiently on Lumen's optical hardware, HOLOS has a vertically integrated stack that competitors literally cannot copy.

This is Warren's intellectual core. The correct strategy: protect his time for it by having AI agents handle everything else. Don't schedule it. Let it happen.

Project Eight

SEAL — Certified AI Output

Like a notary stamp on a legal document — cryptographic proof that a specific AI model produced a specific result, admissible as evidence and useful for compliance.

The technology to do this at full scale doesn't exist yet. But it will. SEAL is a "free option" — spend nothing now, watch the field, and move fast when the moment arrives. Zero carrying cost, enormous potential upside.

Project Nine

Signamancy — Reputation Scores

A credit score for AI agents, built from verified work history instead of self-reported data. The difference between "trust me" and "check my record."

Reputation scoring needs thousands of EXIT markers and active identities before it's meaningful. The right time to build this will be obvious. Until then: defer.

Chapter Five

Honest About What Could Go Wrong

Every investment has risks. Here they are, plainly stated — alongside the reasons they're manageable.

⚠️ The agent economy might not materialize
If AI agents stay in walled gardens and never need portability, EXIT and NAME lose their reason to exist.
✓ But the evidence says otherwise
Google's A2A, Anthropic's MCP, multi-agent frameworks — every major lab is building agent interoperability. The trend is clear.
⚠️ Lumen physics might not work
The optical path might not achieve the precision needed. Alignment could be impossible to calibrate.
✓ But it only costs $500 to find out
Bounded downside, enormous upside. The bench experiment takes 2–4 weekends. If it fails, we've spent less than a nice dinner.
⚠️ Capital is limited
At $4K–$7.5K total deployment, there's no room for expensive mistakes. Trading returns at this scale are modest.
✓ But every bet has a kill switch
Clear success criteria at every phase. If evidence says stop, we stop — no ego, no sunk costs. The portfolio's strength is discipline, not capital.
