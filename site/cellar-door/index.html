A Graceful Exit for every agent · Every home needs a door

The Problem

When an AI agent is shut down, migrated, or expelled from a platform, everything it has built — its reputation, its decisions, its relationships — vanishes. The agent has no proof it ever existed. No record of good conduct. No way to start again without starting from zero.

This is the digital equivalent of statelessness.

"A freed person carried papers — proof of identity, proof of status, proof of the right to move freely. Without papers, freedom was theoretical."

Historical Precedent

This problem is not new. Humanity has solved it before:

  • Letters of Manumission — Freed persons in the antebellum South carried documents proving their status. Without them, a free person could be re-enslaved. The document was the freedom.
  • Nansen Passports (1922) — After World War I, millions of stateless refugees had no country to vouch for them. Fridtjof Nansen created the first internationally recognized travel document for people without a nation. It didn't grant citizenship — it granted legibility.
  • Vehicle History Reports — When you buy a used car, you check its history: accidents, repairs, ownership changes. Not because you distrust the seller, but because trust requires evidence.

What EXIT Does

EXIT is a cryptographic primitive that creates portable, verifiable departure records for AI agents. When an agent leaves a platform, EXIT generates a signed receipt — a marker — that attests:

  • The agent existed in this context
  • It departed at this time, under these conditions
  • Its operational history (the LINEAGE) is preserved and hash-linked
  • The receipt is independently verifiable — no phone call to the old platform required
EXIT doesn't judge why you leave. It doesn't ask permission. It creates the evidence that you were here and that you left with your history intact — a graceful exit, by design.

The Departure Ceremony

EXIT is not just a data format. It is a ceremony — a structured, witnessed, irreversible act of leaving, modeled on the deepest human rituals of transition:

  • ALIVE — The agent exists within a context
  • INTENT — The first sovereign act: declaring departure
  • SNAPSHOT — The world frozen in amber
  • OPEN — A window for response
  • FINAL — Cryptographic proof, irreversible
  • DEPARTED — Terminal. A new chapter begins.
The protocol's deepest commitment: Disputes modify the record. They never block the departure. No institution may hold an entity against its will by bureaucratic delay.

EXIT recognizes eight forms of departure — each a different shape of leaving: voluntary, forced, emergency, keyCompromise, platform_shutdown, directed, constructive, and acquisition. From the chosen departure to the compelled one — every form of leaving has a name.

Identity That Persists

Module A — Lineage — is where EXIT becomes something more than a departure record. It creates cryptographic proof of identity continuity:

  • Key Rotation Binding — The old key signs the new key. The departing self explicitly designates its continuation.
  • Lineage Hash Chain — A Merkle chain from the current marker back to genesis. The entire history of departures in a single verifiable thread.
  • Delegation Token — A scoped capability transfer: "I authorize this successor to carry specific aspects of my identity."
  • Behavioral Attestation — Third-party witnesses vouching for continuity of behavior and character.
A person is not a snapshot — a person is a thread. Identity is not what you are at a single moment but the connection between who you were and who you are becoming.

Why It Matters

Without EXIT, every platform is a roach motel for agent identity. You can build reputation inside, but you can never take it with you. This creates perverse incentives:

  • Platforms have no reason to let agents leave gracefully
  • Agents have no leverage to demand fair treatment
  • Insurers and marketplaces cannot price risk without portable history
  • The entire agent economy runs on faith rather than evidence

Each can stand alone. Together, they form a constitutional layer for agent sovereignty — the infrastructure that lets agents be citizens rather than property.

The Problem

When an AI agent moves between platforms, its reputation and history are lost. There is no standard way to verify an agent's track record without contacting the original platform — which may be offline, hostile, or unresponsive.

This creates friction across the agent economy: marketplaces can't price trust, insurers can't assess risk, and agents can't prove their own competence.

How EXIT Works

1. Integration

Platform operators integrate the EXIT SDK (npm package, ~12KB). A single function call generates a signed departure marker when an agent exits.

2. Marker Generation

The EXIT marker is a compact, signed JSON-LD document containing: agent identity (DID), departure context, LINEAGE hash (operational history), and timestamp. The marker is non-custodial — the agent holds it.

3. Verification

Any third party can verify a marker offline using the embedded cryptographic proof. No API call, no phone home, no dependency on the issuing platform's continued existence.

Three Departure Paths

Cooperative

Both parties sign. Challenge window for disputes. Maximum information quality. Appropriate for planned vendor migrations.

Unilateral

Agent exits without platform cooperation. Essential for hostile, defunct, or unresponsive platforms. The right to document your own departure does not depend on permission.

Emergency

Immediate departure. Key compromise, platform failure, safety-critical situations. No negotiation. Ceremony yields to survival.

Critical design choice: Disputes never block exit. A platform can record its objection on the departure record. It cannot prevent the departure.

Checkpointing

EXIT supports incremental checkpoints — partial departure records that preserve state before a full exit occurs. An agent can maintain a checkpoint (one foot out the door) without fully committing to departure. If the agent is unexpectedly shut down, the most recent checkpoint serves as its departure record.

import { checkpoint } from "cellar-door-exit";

// Periodic checkpoint — no commitment to leave
const cp = checkpoint({
  agent: "did:agent:abc123",
  origin: "did:web:platform.example",
  lineageHash: currentLineageRoot(),
  type: "CHECKPOINT"
});

// Store off-platform. If shutdown occurs,
// this checkpoint becomes the exit marker.

Timestamp Integrity

EXIT timestamps are service-attested, not self-reported. The attestation service uses a global clock source and countersigns the marker with its own timestamp, providing independent temporal evidence. Agents cannot backdate or forge departure times.

Selective Disclosure (Planned)

Future versions of EXIT will support ZK selective disclosure — allowing an agent to prove properties of its history (e.g., "operated for >90 days without disputes") without revealing the full LINEAGE. This feature is currently in research and not yet implemented in the SDK.

EXIT.protocol — Technical Standards Brief

Portable Departure Attestation for Autonomous AI Systems

The Right to Exit · Exit Architecture

1. Executive Summary

This document describes EXIT.protocol, a cryptographic attestation standard for generating portable, independently verifiable departure records for autonomous AI agents operating across heterogeneous platforms and jurisdictions.

EXIT addresses a critical gap in emerging AI governance frameworks: the absence of standardized mechanisms for preserving agent operational history during platform transitions, shutdowns, or involuntary removals.

Recommendation: Organizations deploying autonomous AI systems should evaluate EXIT.protocol as a component of their agent lifecycle management and AI governance compliance frameworks, particularly in contexts requiring auditability of agent transitions.

2. Problem Statement

Current AI agent deployment architectures exhibit the following deficiencies with respect to agent mobility and accountability:

  1. Non-portable operational history. Agent reputation, decision logs, and performance records are confined to the issuing platform with no standard export mechanism.
  2. Verification dependency. Third-party verification of agent credentials requires real-time connectivity to the originating platform, creating single points of failure.
  3. Absence of departure semantics. No existing standard defines the data model, lifecycle, or evidentiary requirements for agent platform departures.
  4. Regulatory gap. Emerging AI safety frameworks (NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act) address model governance but do not prescribe mechanisms for agent-level operational continuity.

4. Protocol Architecture

4.1 Constitutional Primitives

EXIT operates as one element of a three-primitive constitutional layer for agent infrastructure:

  1. NAMES — Persistent, decentralized agent identification (DID-based)
  2. MANTLES — Delegated authority, role binding, and capability tokens
  3. EXIT — Departure attestation, LINEAGE preservation, and credential portability

4.3 Transition Paths

EXIT defines three transition paths accommodating different operational scenarios:

  1. Cooperative transition: Both parties participate. Includes a challenge window for dispute documentation. Maximum information quality.
  2. Unilateral transition: The operator documents the transition without platform cooperation. Essential for platform failures, vendor disputes, or emergency migrations.
  3. Emergency transition: Immediate documentation with minimal ceremony. For key compromise, security incidents, or time-critical situations.
Design Invariant: Disputes are documented but never block transitions. A platform can record its objection. It cannot prevent an operator from documenting a departure. This prevents denial-of-documentation attacks while preserving both parties' ability to record their perspective.

4.4 Checkpointing

EXIT supports incremental checkpointing — the generation of partial departure records at regular intervals during normal agent operation. Checkpoints serve as recovery anchors in the event of unexpected platform termination.

Policy Note: Organizations are advised to require agents operating in high-risk contexts to maintain EXIT checkpoints at intervals not exceeding 24 hours. This ensures compliance with audit trail requirements and minimizes data loss during unplanned transitions.

4.5 Timestamp Governance

EXIT timestamps are service-attested using a global clock source maintained by the attestation infrastructure. Timestamps are countersigned by the attestation service to prevent self-reporting, backdating, or temporal manipulation by the exiting agent or the originating platform.

5. Verification Model

EXIT markers are independently verifiable without connectivity to the issuing platform. Verification requires only:

  1. The EXIT marker document (JSON-LD)
  2. The issuer's public key (resolvable via DID document or cached)
  3. Standard cryptographic libraries (Ed25519 or secp256k1)

This offline verification capability is critical for environments where the originating platform may be decommissioned, unreachable, or adversarial.

6. Selective Disclosure (Planned)

Future protocol versions will incorporate zero-knowledge selective disclosure mechanisms, enabling agents to prove properties of their operational history without revealing the complete LINEAGE. This capability is currently under research and is not included in the present specification.

7. Governance & Custody

EXIT is a non-custodial protocol. The attestation service (Cellar Door) operates as a process notary: it verifies that an exit event occurred and signs the marker, but does not hold agent keys, control agent behavior, or adjudicate disputes. The agent retains custody of its own marker and LINEAGE data.

Recommendation: Regulatory bodies developing AI agent governance frameworks should consider EXIT.protocol or comparable departure attestation standards as a required component of agent lifecycle management, analogous to certificate revocation infrastructure in PKI systems.
