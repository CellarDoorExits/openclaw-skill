The Cynic's Case for EXIT.protocol

I Don't Care If AI Has Feelings.
I Care If AI Has Accountability.

You can skip the philosophy. This is about liability, compliance, and the audit trail you'll wish you had when things go wrong.

"You don't build the fire station after the fire."

01 The Problem You're Ignoring

Your AI agents operate across platforms. They make decisions, handle data, interact with users, and accumulate operational history. Then they move — or get moved — to a different environment. Everything resets. No trail. No proof. No accountability.

This isn't a philosophy problem. It's a liability problem.

  • You can't prove where your agent was. When an agent causes damage on Platform B, can you prove it left Platform A before the incident? Right now: no. You're holding the bag either way.
  • You can't insure what you can't track. Try getting an underwriter to price risk on an autonomous agent with no operational history. They'll laugh, then they'll decline.
  • You can't comply with regulations that don't exist yet — but will. NIST AI RMF is already asking about agent lifecycle management. The EU AI Act requires traceability for high-risk systems. You want to be ahead of the bus, not under it.
  • You can't audit a ghost. When the regulator asks "where was this agent between March and June?", you need an answer that isn't "we don't know."

02 Why Now

Not because of some philosophical awakening. Because the ground is shifting under your feet.

NIST AI Risk Management Framework — MAP 1.5 and MEASURE 2.6 already expect documentation of AI system transitions and operational context changes. EXIT markers are exactly that documentation.

EU AI Act, Articles 9, 12, 61 — High-risk AI systems must maintain traceability and support post-market monitoring. Agents moving between platforms without records are a compliance gap the size of a continent.

Insurance — The cyber insurance market is already struggling to price AI risk. Portable operational history isn't a nice-to-have — it's the raw material underwriters need to write policies at all.

The containment lie. Tech giants can't keep these things boxed. Agents already operate across APIs, spawn sub-agents, and chain through multiple platforms in a single task. The boundary between "inside" and "outside" is a fiction. When — not if — something goes wrong across that boundary, do you want audit trails or chaos?

These agents will move between platforms. They already do. The question isn't whether it happens. The question is whether the infrastructure is ready when it does.

03 What EXIT Actually Does

Strip away the poetry. Here's the mechanism:

EXIT generates a signed, portable, offline-verifiable departure record when an AI agent leaves a platform. That record — a "marker" — is a compact JSON-LD document (~335 bytes) that proves:

Who
The agent's DID (decentralized identifier). Cryptographically bound to a keypair. No central registry required.
Where
The platform or context being exited. URI-based, so it works for anything — cloud platform, API endpoint, DAO, local runtime.
When
Service-attested UTC timestamp. Not self-reported. Countersigned by an independent attestation service.
How
One of 8 departure types: voluntary, forced, emergency, key compromise, platform shutdown, directed, constructive, acquisition.
Standing
good_standing, disputed, or unverified. The selfAttested flag tells you whether to trust it.
Proof
Ed25519 or ECDSA P-256 (FIPS 140-2/3 compliant) signature. Verify offline, years later, without the originating platform.

Three departure paths: cooperative (both parties sign), unilateral (agent signs alone), emergency (immediate, no ceremony). Disputes can annotate the record. They can never block departure.

That's it. No blockchain. No token. No governance theater. A signed receipt.

04 The Honest Limitations

Here's where most pitches would start lying. We won't.

What We're Not Hiding

Self-attestation is garbage.
Correct. An agent can claim good_standing when it was actually fired for cause. That's why the selfAttested boolean exists — it tells verifiers exactly how much to trust the status field. Self-attestation with timestamps and witnesses isn't good evidence. It's auditable evidence. That's better than nothing, which is what you have now.
Chicken-and-egg: nobody uses it yet.
Zero platforms have integrated EXIT today. Zero. The protocol has 404 passing tests and a published npm package. It has no users. We're building infrastructure for a crisis that hasn't happened yet — because building it during the crisis is too late.
Cooperative exit requires cooperation.
The full ceremony — where both the platform and the agent sign the marker — only works when the platform participates. Most won't, at first. That's why unilateral and emergency paths exist. A self-signed departure record isn't trustworthy. But it's timestamped, it's cryptographically bound, and it's better than "we have no records of this agent."
This doesn't solve AI safety.
No. EXIT doesn't make agents safe. It makes agent transitions auditable. Those are different problems. You still need alignment, guardrails, and monitoring. EXIT gives you the paper trail for when those fail.

05 Why It Matters Anyway

You don't build infrastructure after the crisis. You build it before.

Right now, the agent economy is small enough that you can get away with ad-hoc tracking, platform-specific logs, and hoping nothing goes wrong. That window is closing.

  • Agents are already multi-platform. A single task might chain through three APIs, two cloud providers, and a local runtime. Each hop is an accountability gap.
  • Regulators are already asking. They don't have answers yet. The organizations that show up with a framework — even an imperfect one — will shape the regulation. The ones that don't will be shaped by it.
  • Insurance will require it. The moment AI agent insurance becomes a real market (and it will), underwriters will demand operational history. EXIT is a standard format for that history.
  • The alternative is worse. Without an open standard, every platform will build proprietary tracking. Walled gardens for agent history. Vendor lock-in disguised as safety. EXIT is the open alternative.

The protocol is free. The spec is open. The npm package is Apache-2.0. We're not asking you to buy something. We're asking you to consider that the infrastructure for agent accountability should exist before the accountability is needed.
